Title:
Analysis and Characterization of CERN's New Long Bent Silicon Crystals for Channelling Applications at the LHC

Abstract:

The phenomenon of planar channelling in bent silicon crystals enables high effective bending fields for positively charged hadrons, offering an opportunity for fixed-target experiments at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC). By trapping particles within the potential well of highly ordered atomic lattices, macroscopic deflections can be achieved over very compact volumes. This technology is currently being studied for the measurement of the electric and magnetic dipole moments of short-lived charmed baryons. Such measurements require very large deflections which cannot be obtained within manageable distances with current superconducting magnets. While previous studies have validated the performance of crystals bent via established techniques, this thesis focuses on the characterization of a new generation of long bent silicon crystals recently developed internally at CERN.

A detailed study has been performed combining the analysis of experimental data and Monte Carlo simulations to evaluate the feasibility of these new crystals for future experimental setup, e.g. the TWOCRYST project. The evaluation of key channeling parameters such as channeling efficiency and the deflection angle has been performed by analyzing real beam test data. Furthermore, dedicated simulations were developed and executed using Geant4. The comparison between the experimental data and the Geant4 simulations provides a robust assessment of the performance of these long crystals developed at CERN, outlining the potential for their implementation in high-energy accelerator applications.
